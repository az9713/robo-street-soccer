# Robo Street Soccer — Project Brief

Status: final 2v2 implementation and public-release work authorized on 2026-09-06. This brief records the product target; the release receipt must record what is actually verified in the final build.

Build an attractive Windows Unity two-on-two robot soccer prototype. Plausible body movement, consistent physics and responsive fun come first.

Keep all soccer work inside this repository. Preserve the separate tennis project unchanged. Reuse only appropriate inspected assets; exclude credentials, personal logs, transcripts and databases. The user authorizes publication of the finished soccer game to `az9713/robo-street-soccer`; the public package must contain only safe source, documentation, evidence receipts and the portable Windows release.

Small enclosed rebound pitch, two robots per side, no goalkeeper, no offside, first to three. Human controls one robot and local rule-based AI controls the teammate and opponents. Transfer control on actual successful teammate pass reception. BEGINNER is the initial control preset: arrows (optional WASD equivalent) move, Space passes, J or a labeled Shoot button shoots; automatic dribbling and assisted standing tackles on defense only when close AND facing a reachable ball. Tackles require actual foot-ball contact and cooldown; proximity alone never steals. Otherwise selection stays with the controlled robot during live play; no manual switch button is required. Labeled UI buttons provide normal/half/quarter speed, pause/resume and restart; shortcuts are optional. ADVANCED retains contextual pass/switch and shoot/tackle controls with explicit manual defensive switching. GENTLE and BALANCED are selectable difficulty styles, with GENTLE as the default. Difficulty changes opponent behavior and forgiveness only; it does not change physics or scoring. Control presets are independent of difficulty. Beginner keeps pass and shoot separate so AI does not guess the choice. Both presets retain assisted passing and a clear controlled-player ring. Teammate seeks passing space, supports attacks, covers defensively and pursues loose balls. Advanced tactics and persistent learning deferred.

Approved body contact: solid bodies block with slight pushing. Head-on contact slows/blocks with small displacement; glancing contact permits brushing past. Bounded pushing prevents bulldozing. Robots stay upright with lean/recovery reactions. Identical rules apply to both teams and teammates. Body contact can disrupt dribbling but never automatically steals possession.

Approved goal frame: solid posts and crossbar produce speed/angle-dependent rebounds with energy loss and glancing deflections; rebounds remain live for either team. Fast shots must not tunnel through the frame. Score once only after the entire ball crosses the valid mouth inside posts and below crossbar. Frame contact alone or bounce-back before full crossing does not score; frame-to-goal full crossing counts once. Validate posts, crossbar, corner junctions, fast shots, inside/outside near misses, frame-to-goal/frame-to-pitch outcomes and duplicate prevention at all three speeds. A simple behind-goal catching/net boundary suffices; elaborate deforming nets are deferred.

First acceptance milestone: convincing dribble -> pass -> receive -> shoot with one teammate, before adding opponents. Feet must plant without skating. Turning/leaning must be plausible. Foot contact must synchronize with release. Receiving cushions physically without teleporting the ball. Rolling/friction/bounces/collisions must be consistent. Include body-contact validation in this first physics milestone: head-on blocking, glancing contact, sustained push limits, upright recovery and dribble disruption without automatic possession transfer. Validate at normal, half and quarter speed, with close contact inspection at quarter speed.

Approved play style: ground passes and dribbling with modestly lifted shots. Headers, high crosses and bicycle kicks are deferred; natural airborne rebounds still follow physics. The initial physical ball has a 0.11 m radius (0.22 m diameter) and approximately 0.43 kg mass. The earlier 0.22 m radius was a radius/diameter mistake. Current readability tuning intentionally renders a larger visible shell while preserving the 0.11 m physical collision radius; shooting may use the visible boundary as bounded assistance, while dribble/pass/receive/contact evidence uses true physical contact. Prefer color, contrast and shadow for visibility; do not silently change the approved physical radius.

Approved boundary restart: board rebounds stay live. A ball escaping over the boards triggers a brief dead-ball kick-in near its exit point, awarded to the team that did not touch it last. Opponents move back. For the human team, select the restarting robot during dead-ball setup and use Space to pass and resume; AI restarts follow the same physical/contact rules. No throwing animation or extra control. This is a simple arcade rule. Valid goals take precedence, and collision-bug escapes use separately logged game-error recovery rather than blame a player. See SPEC.md for boundary and restart tests.

Approved AI clarification: teammate and opponents use local Unity/C# rule-based state controllers with scored action choices. AI here does not mean GPT, Claude or another trained model. No external inference, API key or learning model is needed. Control presets and selectable Gentle/Balanced difficulty are separate settings; trained models and model selection are deferred.
